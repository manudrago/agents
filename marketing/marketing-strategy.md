# Influential Software — Marketing Strategy

**Status:** draft for review
**Prepared:** July 2026

> **Evidence note:** this strategy is based on publicly available information about the domain estate and the company's public positioning. The direct site fetches were blocked by network policy, so every quantitative claim below must be validated against Search Console, analytics, and CRM data before budget is committed. Where an assumption is doing heavy lifting, it is flagged.

---

## 1. Executive summary

Influential Software's marketing problem is not messaging quality — it is **fragmentation**. Around 25 separate domains split the company's search authority, marketing budget, and brand recognition into slivers, several of them for products in decline.

The recommendation is a **hub-and-spoke consolidation** around `influentialsoftware.com`, combined with a repositioning of the legacy-product microsites from product brochures into **modernisation on-ramps**. AI should be integrated as a thread through existing practices — integration, data/BI, and modernisation — rather than launched as a separate bolt-on offer.

Three moves, in priority order:

1. **Consolidate the estate** — audit, then merge or retire domains into one authoritative site.
2. **Weaponise the legacy portfolio** — market migration and extended support to a stranded, under-served installed base.
3. **Integrate AI into the service catalogue** — AI-ready data, AI-powered integration, Copilot enablement, AI-assisted modernisation, and AI training.

## 2. Where we are

### The estate

The domain portfolio divides into three tiers:

**Tier 1 — Growth technologies.** Power BI, Azure, SAP Analytics Cloud, SuccessFactors, Planning Analytics, app development, Boomi, Jamf/Addigy training. These align with where enterprise budgets are actually moving.

**Tier 2 — Legacy technologies.** BusinessObjects, Cognos, Sybase, PowerHouse, Metalogix, IBM extended support. Superficially these look like dead weight. They are, in fact, the most under-exploited asset in the portfolio (section 4).

**Tier 3 — Generic and utility domains.** Licence sales, CMS development, Java, PHP, .NET, general training. These compete on broad head terms against the entire internet and, in all likelihood, earn very little.

### Why the microsite model now works against us

The exact-match-domain strategy was effective in the 2000s and early 2010s. It no longer is:

- **Search has changed.** Google's E-E-A-T signals and helpful-content updates reward a single authoritative domain with genuine depth over many thin, single-topic sites. Twenty-five domains each earn a fraction of the authority one consolidated domain would.
- **Every domain has a running cost.** Hosting, SSL, CMS patching, and content maintenance multiply across the estate. The predictable result is that most sites go stale — and a visibly stale site actively damages credibility with enterprise buyers evaluating a technology partner.
- **Brand recognition is diluted.** A prospect who reads a Power BI page, a Boomi page, and a Cognos page across three unrelated domains never accumulates a sense of the company behind them.
- **The company has already started fixing this.** `boomi.influentialsoftware.com` and `vena.influentialsoftware.com` use the subdomain model. That is the right pattern; the task is to finish the job.

### What we have going for us

- Founded 1993 — three decades of enterprise delivery credibility.
- Certified partner across Microsoft, IBM, SAP, Salesforce, Boomi, and Apple — unusually broad for a boutique integrator.
- A genuinely impressive client list spanning publishing, energy, retail, insurance, and central/local government.
- 12,000+ people trained with high satisfaction — a training arm most competitors lack.
- Deep expertise in legacy platforms that almost nobody else still supports.

## 3. Positioning

**Target buyers:** IT directors, CTOs, heads of digital transformation, and data/BI leads in UK mid-market and enterprise organisations — particularly those running ageing enterprise estates they know need to change.

**Positioning statement:**

> Influential Software helps UK enterprises modernise the systems they depend on — without betting the business on a big-bang rewrite. Thirty years of enterprise delivery, partner-certified across the platforms you run today and the ones you're moving to.

**What makes this defensible:** most consultancies sell either legacy support *or* modern platform work. Influential does both, which means it can meet a client where they actually are — running BusinessObjects and wanting Power BI — and own the whole journey. That dual capability is the core differentiator and should lead the messaging.

**Tone:** confident, plain-spoken, evidence-led. Enterprise buyers are sceptical of transformation rhetoric; specificity beats adjectives.

## 4. Strategic move: legacy as a migration engine

This is the highest-return, lowest-competition opportunity in the portfolio.

**The insight:** organisations still running BusinessObjects, Cognos, Sybase, or PowerHouse are stranded. Their platforms are approaching or past end-of-life, vendor support is thinning, internal skills have retired out of the business, and they are under pressure to modernise. They are actively searching for help — and almost nobody is marketing to them, because most agencies chase the modern-platform keywords instead.

**The reframe:** every legacy property shifts from *"we support X"* to *"we support X, and we'll get you off it safely when you're ready."*

**Why it converts:** extended support opens the relationship at low risk to the client. Migration is where the revenue is. The client gets continuity instead of a cliff edge; we get a multi-year engagement that ends in modern-platform work — which is Tier 1 revenue acquired through Tier 2 marketing.

**Target search intent (validate volumes before committing):**

- "Business Objects migration" / "BusinessObjects to Power BI"
- "Cognos end of life" / "Cognos to Power BI migration"
- "Sybase end of support" / "Sybase migration UK"
- "PowerHouse migration" / "PowerHouse support UK"
- "IBM extended support UK"

These are low-volume, high-intent, low-competition terms — the ideal profile for a consultancy with long deal cycles and high deal values. One qualified enquiry can justify a quarter of content investment.

## 5. AI: integrate, don't bolt on

`influentialsoftware.ai` already exists, and the company's public positioning already references using AI to optimise processes and applying generative AI to integration. The intent is there; it needs to become a concrete, sellable service catalogue.

**The principle:** AI should be a thread running through existing practices, not a separate business unit. The credibility comes from the delivery track record, not from claiming AI expertise in the abstract.

**Five service lines, in recommended launch order:**

1. **AI-ready data foundations.** The BI and analytics practice — Power BI, SAP Analytics Cloud, Cognos, Planning Analytics — is precisely what enterprises need in place before AI produces anything trustworthy. "Your AI is only as good as the data underneath it" is a message this company can make with authority, and it converts existing capability into AI-adjacent demand without over-claiming.
2. **AI-powered integration.** Boomi has been building AI agents into its platform. As a Boomi partner, this is a differentiated offer very few UK integrators can make credibly.
3. **Copilot and Azure OpenAI enablement.** Fits the existing Microsoft and Azure practice, and is what mid-market clients are actually asking about right now. Highest near-term demand of the five.
4. **AI-assisted legacy modernisation.** AI materially accelerates code analysis and migration off Sybase, PowerHouse, and legacy .NET estates. This connects the Tier 2 strategy directly to the AI story — the same client, the same journey.
5. **AI adoption training.** With 12,000+ people trained, extending into enterprise AI adoption and governance training is a fast, low-risk line extension for the training arm.

**On the `.ai` domain:** make it a focused campaign landing property that feeds the main site, or redirect it to an AI hub on `influentialsoftware.com`. Do not build a twenty-sixth standalone island — that would repeat the exact mistake this strategy exists to correct.

**Guardrails:** every AI claim must be backed by a named delivery capability or a client outcome. In a market saturated with AI marketing, an enterprise buyer's default assumption is that AI messaging is noise. Specific, unglamorous, verifiable claims will outperform ambitious ones.

## 6. Channel plan

The buyer is a UK IT decision-maker with a long, committee-driven buying cycle. Channels are prioritised accordingly.

**Organic search — primary.** The consolidation work and the migration-intent keyword strategy compound over time and match how these buyers research. This is where the majority of effort belongs.

**LinkedIn — primary social.** Company page plus practice-lead voices. Enterprise IT buyers are demonstrably present; no other social channel justifies the effort for this audience.

**Case studies — primary proof.** The client list is a significant underused asset. Convert named engagements into structured case studies (subject to client approval) — challenge, approach, measurable outcome. Enterprise buyers discount everything except evidence of comparable delivery.

**Email nurture — essential given cycle length.** Someone searching "Cognos end of life" today may buy in nine months. Without nurture, that lead is lost between first touch and budget approval.

**Paid media — supporting, targeted.** LinkedIn Ads against job-title and company-size audiences; Google Ads only on high-intent migration and support terms where organic hasn't yet ranked. Not a volume play — these are precision buys against small, valuable audiences.

**Partner co-marketing — underused.** Certified partner status with Microsoft, IBM, SAP, Salesforce, Boomi, and Apple typically carries co-marketing funds, directory listings, and referral routes. Cheapest credible reach available; audit what each partnership entitles us to.

## 7. Sequenced plan

**Phase 1 — Audit and decide (weeks 1–4).** Pull Search Console, analytics, and CRM data for every domain. Complete the keep/merge/kill worksheet. Produce a redirect map and a one-page sign-off summary with estimated annual saving.

*Deliverable:* completed domain audit, agreed migration order.

**Phase 2 — Consolidate (weeks 5–16).** Execute migrations in ascending order of risk: retire the dormant generic domains first, then merge Tier 1 growth properties, then the high-traffic legacy properties last. Page-level 301s throughout; track rankings weekly for 90 days after each move.

*Deliverable:* a consolidated estate of roughly 5–8 properties instead of 25.

**Phase 3 — Reposition legacy (weeks 8–20, overlapping).** Rewrite the legacy properties around migration and extended support. Build the migration content cluster: one authoritative page per migration path, supported by end-of-life explainers and comparison content.

*Deliverable:* migration content cluster live and indexed.

**Phase 4 — Launch AI services (weeks 12–24, overlapping).** Define and publish the five AI service lines on the main site. Resolve the `.ai` domain's role. Brief sales on positioning and qualification.

*Deliverable:* AI hub live, sales enabled.

**Phase 5 — First integrated campaign (weeks 20+).** "Legacy BI to AI-ready analytics", targeting the BusinessObjects and Cognos installed base. Search plus LinkedIn plus nurture, converging on a migration assessment offer.

*Deliverable:* campaign live with measurement in place.

## 8. Measurement

Set the baseline during Phase 1 — otherwise consolidation gains will be unprovable and the strategy will be judged on anecdote.

**Leading indicators (weekly/monthly):** organic impressions and clicks on the consolidated domain; rankings for the target migration keyword set; enquiry volume by service line; LinkedIn engagement rate and follower growth.

**Lagging indicators (quarterly):** marketing-sourced pipeline value; opportunities by service line, split legacy-support versus migration versus AI; cost per qualified enquiry; win rate on migration engagements.

**Efficiency:** annual hosting and maintenance cost of the estate, before versus after.

**Expected pattern:** consolidation typically causes a short-term dip in aggregate traffic before a larger recovery. Agree this expectation with stakeholders **before** starting, or Phase 2 will be judged a failure at week 8.

## 9. Risks

| Risk | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Traffic loss during migration | High | Page-level 301 maps, phased rollout lowest-risk-first, weekly rank tracking, rollback plan per migration |
| Stakeholder panic at the expected post-migration dip | High | Agree the expected curve and review points in advance |
| Legacy revenue cannibalised by migration messaging | Medium | Position migration as *when you're ready* — extended support remains the entry offer, not a lost sale |
| AI claims outrunning delivery capability | High | Every claim tied to a named capability or client outcome; solutions architect signs off service-line copy |
| Client approval blocking case studies | Medium | Start approval conversations early; anonymised sector versions as fallback |
| Trademark exposure on product-name domains | Medium | Legal review of product-named domains during the audit |
| Consolidation stalls at 60% complete | Medium | Single named owner, migration order agreed up front, progress reviewed fortnightly |
